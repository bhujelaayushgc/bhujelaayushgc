# Hi, I'm Aayush 👋

I enjoy engineering systems. Software just happens to be the medium I’ve spent the most time working in.

Over the past decade, I’ve worked across backend architecture, distributed systems, cloud infrastructure, real-time platforms, data pipelines, geospatial processing, engineering operations, and AI-enabled software development.

I don’t really identify as a programmer.

I identify as a builder and engineer.

Programming is one way to solve problems. Engineering is understanding the problem, exploring constraints, making good technical decisions, designing systems that last, and executing with confidence.

Whether I’m designing a distributed platform, an AI-assisted workflow, or even planning a home like a long-lived system, I tend to think in terms of constraints, tradeoffs, maintainability, scalability, and what future-me will regret.

## How I think about engineering

Good engineering starts before the first line of code.

I enjoy taking ambiguous ideas, understanding the constraints, and turning them into systems that people can build, operate, and rely on.

Sometimes that means designing a backend system. Sometimes it means reviewing an implementation plan. Sometimes it means improving infrastructure, debugging production behavior, or helping AI understand a complex codebase well enough to become a useful engineering partner.

The technology changes. The engineering principles stay surprisingly consistent.

---

## Engineering with AI

Software engineering has changed.

Today, writing code is only one part of building software. I treat AI as another engineering tool, alongside architecture, testing, documentation, observability, automation, and code review.

I use AI throughout the engineering lifecycle, from exploring ideas and planning architecture to implementation, debugging, documentation, and operational analysis.

The models will change.
The tools will change.
Good engineering doesn’t.
